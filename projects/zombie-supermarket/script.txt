Imagine walking into a supermarket for a few groceries. You planned to stay fifteen minutes. Then the screaming starts. Outside, people are attacking each other in the parking lot. They're not fighting. They're biting. You run back inside, and the doors close behind you. You think you're safe. You're wrong. For the next thirty days, this supermarket will be your prison. And not everyone inside will survive.

Day one. Your name is Alex. The alarm starts ringing. Through the glass, you see dozens of infected people chasing anyone they can catch. You shove shopping carts against the doors, and three strangers help you block the entrance. Inside, there are seven more people. A mother and her little daughter. An old man. Two teenagers. A store employee. And a quiet man in a black jacket. Your phone has no signal. Outside, hundreds of infected surround the building. There's no way out.

Day three. You divide the food. Canned beans, water, cereal. At first, everyone shares. But the supplies vanish too fast. That night, you hear something in the storage room. Empty boxes on the floor. Someone has been stealing food. Then you see a bloody handprint on the door. And something knocks from the other side.

Day seven. The employee notices one of the teenagers hiding a bite under his sleeve. He's infected. The man in the black jacket grabs a metal bat. The boy begs. He was bitten saving his brother. You have to decide. Protect him, or protect everyone? Before anyone answers, the boy lunges at the old man. Chaos. You pull the little girl away. The danger is inside now.

Day ten. The infected boy is locked in a storage room. But the crowd outside has found the entrance. Every night, they pound on the glass. The barricade is cracking. You carry the food up to the office above the store and find an emergency exit. It leads straight into the parking lot. Full of infected. You wait.

Day fifteen. At midnight, every light goes out. The freezers stop humming. Red emergency lights start flashing. You hear footsteps in the aisles. A shadow moves between the shelves. One of them got in through the loading dock. It attacks the man in the black jacket. You slam the dock door shut. When you turn around, he's gone.

Day twenty-one. Food is almost gone. The mother and her daughter are getting weaker. You go to the emergency exit and freeze. The man in the black jacket is alive. He's been hiding in the maintenance tunnels with a radio. A military rescue point is outside the city. Helicopters in two days. But he says there's only room for three. Then a crash shakes the building. The front doors are down. There's no more time.

Day thirty. You open the emergency exit. The parking lot is full of abandoned cars, and the infected are everywhere. You grab the little girl's hand and run. One of them sees you. Then a dozen. You reach an empty delivery truck. The employee gets it started. Everyone piles in, and you speed away. For the first time in a month, you feel free. Then the radio crackles. "All evacuation centers have been lost. Do not approach the military base." The little girl whispers, "Are we safe now?" You don't answer. Because the employee is sitting quietly in the back, holding his wrist. And there's a fresh bite mark.
